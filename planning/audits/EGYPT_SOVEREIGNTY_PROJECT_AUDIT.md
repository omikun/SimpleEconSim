# Tel el Kebir defense checkpoint

Run each condition in its own process: `MPLCONFIGDIR=/private/tmp/coc-mpl venv/bin/python audit_egypt_sovereignty_project.py 0 low.json` and then `... audit_egypt_sovereignty_project.py 500 high.json`. The project pays 70 treasury units to three local workers, needs two staffed turns, and consumes five units of wages plus six actual public maize units in crew meals per operating turn. Without either input it cannot provide a battle bonus. The original workers receive both wages and the reserved meals; public stores fall accordingly, competing directly with household relief.

When staffed, supplied and unoccupied, the works add up to 55 to the existing Tel el Kebir trench strength. A real battle uses that strength in its defense multiplier and records the project contribution; the works take damage and can be repaired through a major decision. Britain includes the prepared line in its required expedition budget and in the value of Egyptian diplomatic offers. On a landing order, Britain can sustain the blockade, withdraw when it cannot fund the canal march, sail via Port Said and Ismailia, or attack Alexandria if canal access is denied. These are evaluated from current cash, route access, fort readiness and the live claim. French fleet support continues to use its separately funded authorization.

The current full replays end without a naval approach even after adding 500 British treasury units: the reported British protest is 10/10 by turn 8, above the intervention limit. This is a real balance blocker, not proof the defense investment deterred a plausible opponent. Focused tests do show the causal paths: at a marginal treasury Britain changes from intervention to withdrawal when the line becomes ready; a forced funded landing follows the canal route and applies its 55-point bonus; denying canal access instead sends Britain to Alexandria. Phase 6 must tune foreign domestic pressure and replay the full historical branches before claiming the campaign is complete.
