# Alexandria revenue office checkpoint

Run `MPLCONFIGDIR=/private/tmp/coc-mpl venv/bin/python audit_egypt_fiscal_project.py output.json`. The office costs 60 treasury units paid to three Alexandria workers, needs three staffed turns, and costs three in operating wages each turn. It moves 40% of **actual net local tax and tariff receipts** into Cairo's treasury. Local governments retain 60%. Existing Caisse assignments intercept their legally specified share first. The office changes neither tax rates nor the amount taxpayers pay.

The receipt ledger names the payer class, collecting government, affected districts, Egyptian treasury recipient, local remainder, and transferred amount. A focused test puts a 10-unit tax receipt through an active assignment: 2.5 units reduce the bond claim and reach creditors, 3 move to Cairo, and 4.5 remain local. There is no extra receipt. One turn after office completion, the Caisse responds according to its outstanding claim and legal assignment. Without an assignment it demands accounts; publishing raises confidence but does not pay the debt, while refusal lowers confidence and can escalate a defaulted claim.

In one full replay, the payment-first route made no national transfers; the office route logged 549 units transferred over the campaign. By turn 11 the recorded Cairo treasury was 72 on the payment-first route and 240 with the office, and both had reduced the claim from 600 to 200. These are illustrative paths, **not** a controlled difference: repeated worlds in one process still vary. The reliable causal evidence is the per-receipt transfer ledger and the unit test. The office also costs an early major action and 60 initial units, and it reduces local government budgets; future balance work must test the social cost of that centralization and whether the office dominates other projects.
