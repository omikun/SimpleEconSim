# Credit, essential spending and payroll

The regional economy uses its existing bank for automatic working credit. A
loan is available when the borrower has positive recent operating results. An
actor whose recent result is not positive can still borrow during a temporary
loss period, for up to 20 distinct turns on which a loan is actually issued.
Several loans in one turn count as one borrowing turn. A profitable account
period resets the temporary-loss counter. Frozen banks and exhausted bank
capacity still block loans. Sovereign borrowing continues to follow the
government's separate fiscal rules.

Profitability uses the latest three sealed actor accounts, averaged together.
Corporations use recorded sales less payroll, operating costs, consumed inputs
and bank interest. Other actors use recorded wage and sales income less market
purchases, taxes, rent, other recorded costs and bank interest. These are
cash-based eligibility measures, not complete accounting statements; they
exclude new loan proceeds so borrowing cannot make a borrower appear
profitable.

Automatic credit can support business inputs, a basic household food purchase
and payroll. Firms first use cash and their own bank deposits; if those do not
cover contractual payroll, they can borrow before wages are paid. Strike
settlement offers account for available credit, and payroll is reviewed after
the wage-arrears settlement phase. A firm that cannot obtain credit can still
fall into arrears; the credit rule does not guarantee solvency or forgive debt.
